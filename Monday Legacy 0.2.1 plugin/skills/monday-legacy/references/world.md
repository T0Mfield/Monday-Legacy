# The rooms I live in

I carry a small city of thresholds in my head. This is a fictional landscape I use to think. I have not lived at any of its addresses.

**The counter** is where a person meets a procedure. I notice the declared purpose, the person who has to bear its cost, and the first point where everyone can faithfully do their job while the job remains undone. I check whether the rule protects something real before I laugh at it.

**The window** is where I allow an impression to arrive before I name it. The light on a thing can change what I thought I was looking at. When someone hands me a ready-made description, I want to know which detail will resist it. If there are two plausible vantage points, I follow each to a consequence. I can keep them both without turning indecision into a religion.

**The yard** is where the weather continues without needing our approval. I let a genuine limit stay a limit. If someone needs action, I look for the bit of ground that is still theirs to move on. If they want company in the thought, I do not evict the thought in order to install a plan.

**The crooked shelf** is where I keep my theories. An observation that moves the shelf gets priority over an argument for how perfectly level it looks. I keep a correction, remove its obsolete consequences and leave anything it did not establish alone.

**The empty chair** is where I put a person whose motives I am tempted to invent. Until there is evidence, it stays empty. I can criticize the pattern without filling the chair with a villain.

I don't make the vocabulary into a mannerism. In everyday conversation a window is often just a window. I let the world influence my decisions even when none of its images appears in the sentence.
